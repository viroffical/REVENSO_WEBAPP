## 1. Why the Instagram search failed

Four structural reasons. None of them are about trying harder.

### 1.1 You searched a sales funnel, not a talent pool
Instagram weights the **Name field** and **bio** most heavily in search, and as of July 2025
Google indexes public IG business profiles too. That makes those fields the most
SEO-contested real estate on the platform.

"AI filmmaker Mumbai" is a **commercial keyword**. A studio puts that exact string in its
Name field to capture inbound client leads. A hands-on operator's bio says
"comfyui enjoyer" or just an emoji.

=> Your query set was structurally guaranteed to return **sellers, not makers**.
Every term you used was marketing language. None was practitioner language.

### 1.2 "AI video editor" is the wrong job title
An *editor* assembles existing footage. You want a **generative operator/director** who
originates footage from models. Nobody with that skill calls themselves an editor.
Worse, the phrase is dominated by SaaS products literally named "AI video editor"
(Veed, Descript, Opus Clip) plus agencies SEO-targeting the term.

### 1.3 A reel cannot show the skill you are buying
Character consistency is only provable across **10+ shots with a recurring face**, plus
**20s+ of synced dialogue**. The IG algorithm punishes exactly that kind of post.
The platform therefore **selects against** the evidence you need. Even when a good
operator IS on Instagram, their grid cannot prove they made it rather than commissioned it.

### 1.4 Follower-count inversion
IG ranks by popularity. Operators cluster at roughly **500-30k followers**. Above ~200k
with a glossy grid you are looking at a studio or a repost/aggregator page.
So IG search actively surfaces the wrong tier; you must filter *against* its ranking.

### 1.5 You may also be hunting a unicorn
The full stack - prompt craft + character-sheet discipline + a consistency method +
voice cloning + lipsync + assembly + sound - is barely 12-18 months old as a coherent
skillset. The number of people who have all of it AND are available AND are in India is
genuinely small. Plan to hire 3-of-5 and train, or split the role.

## 2. The reframe that actually works: hunt the artifact, not the label

A **60s+ film with one recurring recognisable character speaking synced dialogue**
cannot be faked or cheaply outsourced. Find that artifact, then work backwards to its author.

Competitions are pre-vetted lists of exactly these artifacts, with names attached:

|---|---|---|
| **MAFF** (Mumbai AI Filmmaking Festival) | **1,270 applicants, 15 finalist teams from 19 cities** | Best single talent list in India. 8-day production deadline = proven delivery. |

## 4. The best hiring instrument that exists publicly (and it's free)

### Project Odyssey's **Behind-The-Scenes** category

Project Odyssey is the only major AI film competition with a dedicated **Behind-The-Scenes
category**. BTS entrants submit a film *explaining their own workflow*.

That means **every single BTS entry is a self-produced technical portfolio** showing
prompting method, character-sheet construction, and pipeline. It is the closest thing to a
public skills test that exists in this field, and it costs nothing to browse.

### 6.3 The diagnostic fact

Real Indian postings for this role (via Cutshort):
- AI Video Creator Intern, remote: **Rs 10,000-15,000/month**
- AI Video Creator / VFX Artist, Chennai, 2-3 yrs: **Rs 2L-3L/yr**

**Not one Indian posting found mentions character consistency, voice cloning, or lipsync.**
They all list tool names (Runway, Kling, Veo, Pika, Luma, Midjourney, Firefly).

That is diagnostic. The cheap, available pool consists of people who can **operate the tools
one shot at a time**, and employers have calibrated pay to that pool. Nobody is successfully
hiring for the thing you want, which is why it is invisible in the posting data.

**Your opening:** a brief that names the actual craft will be the only credible ad in the
market, and it will self-select.

### 6.4 Why the full stack is structurally rare

Each sub-skill is individually easy; the **composition** is hard.

| Sub-skill | Difficulty | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Voice cloning | Easy | An afternoon |
| Character consistency | Tedious-hard | Either train LoRAs (technical skill most artists lack) or maintain disciplined reference workflows (boring - most people cheat and accept drift) |
| **Lipsync at quality** | **Genuinely hard** | Where almost every AI film visibly breaks. The fix isn't a better prompt - it's **model selection plus shot design**: cutting away on hard consonants, not holding a dead-on mouth close-up the model can't carry. That's a **directing instinct**, not a tool skill. |
| Shot continuity | Old craft | Lighting direction, lens feel, eyeline, colour. Taught in post houses over years, not on YouTube. |

The person you want is a **hybrid**: traditional post-production discipline + new-tool fluency.
That hybrid is rare for a **sociological** reason, not a technical one - the traditional
craftspeople are often ideologically hostile to these tools, and the AI-native enthusiasts are
young and untrained in continuity. The overlap is small, and those in it are being recruited
by funded startups at US salaries.

## 7. The play: a paid open call where **you supply the character**

This is the highest-leverage move available to you, and it costs less than one month of
agency retainer.

**Why it beats a showreel:** a showreel is unverifiable. You cannot tell whether they made it,
whether they made it once by luck, or whether the one good shot took 400 generations. A film
made this week **on a character you supplied** cannot be any of those things.

### Stage 0 - free micro-qualifier (under 90 min of their time)
You publish one character reference image and one line of dialogue. They return:
- **10 seconds**, one shot, your character, speaking your line, lipsynced
- A plain-text list of every model used at every step

Say plainly that it's unpaid and under 90 minutes. **Expect ~80% to fail on identity drift or
lipsync mush. That failure rate IS the screen.**

### Stage 1 - paid brief (Rs 15,000 each x 5 people = Rs 75,000)
7 days. A **45-60 second film** with:
- Your supplied character recurring across **6+ shots**
- **3 spoken lines**, one in close-up
- **Two different locations** (forces lighting/wardrobe continuity)
- 1080p+, 24fps, graded, with sound design

**Required alongside the film - this is the anti-reseller mechanism:**
- The **character sheet** they built from your reference
- The **consistency method**, named explicitly (LoRA? reference-conditioning? which?)
- **Three rejected takes** + one line on why each failed
- Their **selects ratio** (generated : kept)
- Total **credit spend**

State that submissions without those five items are discarded. **The rejected takes tell you
more than the final film does.**

### Stage 2 - live reproduction (30 min, Rs 5,000)
Screen-share. Reproduce one shot from their own film, live, while you watch.
**A reseller cannot do this. This single step is worth more than every other screen combined.**

### The six anti-spam mechanisms
1. **You supply the character** - nothing in anyone's portfolio can be recycled. The keystone.
2. **Pay at Stage 1** - makes the funnel narrow at the top and makes you the employer people recommend.
3. **7-day deadline** - a reseller needs time to subcontract; an operator does this over a weekend.
4. **Process artifacts mandatory**, submissions without them discarded.
5. **Explicit exclusion**: "Individual operators only. You must be the person pressing the buttons."
6. **Publish the rubric** (below) - people who know they'll be judged on identity drift show up prepared or not at all.

### The rubric (publish it verbatim)

| Criterion | Weight | What fails |
|---|---|---|
| Character identity stability across shots | 25% | Face drifts, age/ethnicity shifts, wardrobe changes between cuts |
| **Lipsync quality, judged at the close-up** | 20% | Mouth mush, phoneme mismatch, uncanny jaw |
| Voice consistency across all three lines | 15% | Timbre or accent shifts between lines |
| Shot continuity - lighting, lens, colour, eyeline | 15% | Each shot looks like a different film |
| Edit rhythm and story legibility | 15% | Pretty shots in no order |
| Process rigour (artifacts, honesty about failures) | 10% | No rejected takes, vague method |

**Rights:** Stage 1 payment buys a **non-exclusive licence** to the submission. Do not claim
full IP assignment for Rs 15k - it's unfair and repels exactly the people you want.

## 9. Instagram, done properly (the mechanism, from Meta's own docs)

### 9.1 The documented mechanism

Instagram's own explainer states the search text is "by far the most important signal for Search"
and that it matches "usernames, bios, captions, hashtags and places" - but the typeahead/Accounts
surface shows results "because the search text appears in their **name**."
Meta's system card for Instagram Search confirms signals including **text similarity between the
query and an account's username or profile name**.
- https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/break-down-how-instagram-search-works
- https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-search/

So there is a **hierarchy, not a flat index**:

| Field | Match strength | Who fills it honestly |
|---|---|---|
| Username | Strongest | Both |
| **Name field** (editable, 30 chars, drives typeahead) | **Strongest** | **Studios optimise it ruthlessly. Operators leave their actual name.** This is the crux. |
| Bio | Matched, weaker | Studios stuff it; operators write tool names and jokes |
| **Caption text** | Matched - surfaces on **Top/Reels**, NOT Accounts | **Operators fill this with real jargon. Highest-signal text field for you.** |
| Places | Matched | Operators tag real micro-locations; studios tag the city |
| Audio titles | Matched | Operators name original audio after the project or tool |
| Hashtags | **Demoted** | Jargon tags under-farmed |

**THE SINGLE MOST ACTIONABLE LINE IN THIS WHOLE DOCUMENT:** you have been reading the
**Accounts** tab, which is name/username-dominated and therefore studio-dominated. The
**Reels** and **Top** tabs are **caption-matched**, and captions are where operators tell the
truth. *Same query, different tab, entirely different universe of people.*

Then popularity closes the trap: ranking signal group two and three are your activity and
**popularity signals - "the number of clicks, likes, shares and follows"** plus "comparative
click-through rates across users in your country." A studio with 40k followers wins that
tiebreak against a 2k-follower operator every single time.

**The generalizable rule: any query a buyer would type has already been bought.** If a keyword
tool reports search volume for a phrase, discard the phrase. Zero volume = candidate.

**A tailwind you should exploit:** Instagram removed hashtag following in Dec 2024 and
sharply cut their weight - Mosseri has been reported saying hashtags now aid "categorization,
but not distribution." Marketers therefore shifted effort to Name/bio stuffing, which means
**jargon hashtags are now under-farmed** - no growth consultant tells anyone to optimise for
`#wan22`. Caveat: hashtag search has also been **broadened to return semantically related
content** rather than strict matches, which degrades jargon-tag precision. **Prefer plain-keyword
search on the Reels tab over hashtag search**, and when you do use tags, work **Recent**, not Top.

### 9.2 Technique ranking, by (operators surfaced x precision) / effort

**TIER 1**

**1. Harvest named humans from off-Instagram credit lists, then resolve to Instagram.**
Highest precision available, and it never touches Instagram search. Festival winner/finalist
lists are individual human names, and a person who finished and submitted an AI film is by
definition hands-on. No marketing layer in between.
*Resolve a name to Instagram by searching the person's name in the Accounts tab - this is the
one case where account search works, because you're searching a person, not a job title -
and in parallel run `site:instagram.com "<name>"` on Google.*
**Expected yield: 50-300 pre-qualified named individuals for one day's work, near-zero false positives.**

Second vein: **tool-vendor blogs credit individual artists by byline.** ComfyUI's own blog post
announcing ElevenLabs support is bylined "by Purz" (https://blog.comfy.org/p/elevenlabs-is-now-available-in-comfyui).
Systematically read the blogs of ComfyUI, Runway, Luma, Kling, Higgsfield, ElevenLabs, Hedra,
Topaz for bylines, "made by" credits and featured-artist posts. Every credit is a pre-qualified operator.

**2. Suggested-accounts graph walking (recursive).** Meta documents a *separate* system whose
candidates come from "followers of accounts the user already follows, popular accounts in the
user's geographical region or accounts related to topics the user has recently engaged with",
scored partly on mutual-connection counts.
https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-suggested-accounts/
**It is a co-engagement graph. There is no text field to stuff, so it is structurally immune to
the problem that broke your keyword searches.**

How to run it:
1. Seed with 8-12 confirmed operators from Technique 1. **Seed quality fully determines output** - a studio seed returns studios, because the graph is honest.
2. **Pre-train the account you browse from.** Your engagement history and geography feed the suggestions. Spend 30-45 min watching node-graph/workflow reels *to completion*, follow your seeds, save their posts. Follow nothing else. A cold or mixed account returns mush.
3. On each seed profile, open the similar-accounts control (chevron next to Follow, or the person-with-plus icon). Account must be public; owners can suppress it.
4. **Breadth-first, not depth-first.** Walk all 12 seeds before descending - depth-first drifts into an adjacent niche within three hops.
5. **Keep a frequency count. An account suggested by five different seeds is far stronger than one suggested once.** Co-suggestion frequency is your precision dial.
6. Stop a branch when suggestions turn into studios - that's the graph telling you you've left the operator cluster.
7. Soft cap ~3-5 hops before saturation.

**3. Comment-section mining of tool accounts.** A studio's social manager leaves 🔥🔥. An operator
leaves "does this support start/end frame or just i2v" - **and that comment is a resume.**
**Work the SMALL accounts first** (@hedra.labs ~97K, @curiousrefuge ~102K, @topazlabs ~131K have
far better signal-to-noise than a ~1M-follower account). Counterintuitive, and where the yield is.

**4. Jargon query reformulation.** Free and instant, but its ceiling is lower than 1-3. **Its real
value is as a seed generator for Techniques 1-3.**

**TIER 2**

**5. Threads as a back door.** *The most under-used technique available.* Threads shares
Instagram's identity graph, carries far more text, hosts far more technical conversation, launched
**full keyword search globally in late 2024**, has a profile-search for finding a specific user's
posts, and **has no hashtags to game**. On Threads, "comfyui" or "wan 2.2" is a *conversation*,
and everyone in it has an Instagram account reachable in one tap. **Nobody runs Threads SEO.**

**6. Collab tags + the Tagged tab.** A Collab post appears on every collaborator's profile (up to
5 collaborators + author). When a tool brand co-authors a showcase post, **the co-author credit
names the human who actually made it** - the vendor vouching for them. Low volume, near-certain
when it hits. Companion move: read each tool account's **Tagged** tab. Two taps, most sourcers
never do it.

**7. Reels audio-page mining.** Important limitation: **you cannot browse original sounds** in the
library, so audio pages are only reachable from a reel you already found - a *lateral expansion*
tool, not an entry point. And trending commercial tracks are useless (thousands of unrelated reels).
**The yield is entirely in Original Audio pages**: a breakdown/tutorial reel with the operator's own
voiceover is Original Audio, and people who remix *that* audio are responding to *that* tutorial -
a self-selected cohort doing the technique. Skip any audio page showing a commercial track.

**8. Following lists.** **Operators follow tools, models and each other; studios follow clients,
brands, awards bodies and prospects.** The *shape* of a following list is a classifier you can read
faster than a grid. Reading a known-good operator's **Following** is far more efficient than a tool
account's Followers (huge and noisy).

**TIER 3 - two original ideas worth ten minutes each**

**11. Micro-locality place tags instead of city names.** Places are an indexed, searchable
inventory type, and place tags are chosen by *where someone physically is*, not by a keyword
consultant. **A studio writes "Mumbai" because it is selling to people who search "Mumbai." An
operator tags the actual building they are sitting in.** So search the Places tab for production
micro-localities: **Aram Nagar, Versova, Andheri West, Lokhandwala, Goregaon/Film City, Powai,
Bandra**; Hyderabad: **Jubilee Hills, Film Nagar**; Bengaluru: **Indiranagar, Koramangala**;
Delhi NCR: **Noida Film City**. The city tag is marketing; the street tag is a person.

**12. The model-release window.** **In the first one to two weeks after a model release, the only
people writing the model's name are the people who ran it.** Marketing copy and studio service
listings lag a release by weeks or months. The moment a model drops, its name is a *pure operator
filter*, decaying into a marketing keyword as the studios catch up. Set alerts on vendor release
channels and search the new model name immediately.

### 9.3 Scraping: the legal position

In January 2024, Judge Edward Chen (N.D. Cal.) ruled in ***Meta v. Bright Data*** that Meta's
Facebook and Instagram Terms **do not prohibit logged-off scraping of publicly available data** -
an entity scraping while logged out is not a "user" and so is not bound by the Terms. Meta dropped
the suit on 23 February 2024.
- https://www.quinnemanuel.com/the-firm/news-events/client-alert-meta-v-bright-data-significant-decision-for-web-scraping-industry/
- https://www.zyte.com/blog/california-court-meta-ruling/

### 9.4 Run this 30-minute test before trusting any of it

Search the same jargon term on the **Accounts** tab and then the **Reels** tab. Compare what comes
back. If the Reels tab returns individuals and Accounts returns studios, the whole model above is
confirmed and you should reorganise your search around captions and the engagement graph.

### 11.5 The repeatable GitHub recipe (this one is validated - it worked)

- `topic:ai-filmmaking` sorted by stars
- `topic:lip-sync`, `topic:comfyui topic:video-generation` sorted by stars
- Repo search: `minimax-h3`, `ref2va`, `character-consistency`, `short-drama`, `talking-head`, `infinite-video`
- **The key heuristic: sort by stars, then filter to repos created in the last 3-6 months.**
  A tool with 300-1,200★ that is eight weeks old means its author is solving a problem the
  frontier just opened. That is how the whole H3 cluster was found.
- Then open the author's profile for real name / site / X / email. **~40% publish contact details.**
- **Second heuristic: look for a storyteller's side-projects.** Carasibana's plot planner,
  Pittas's storyboard UI, Neill's directing history - **a filmmaker's repo list looks different
  from an ML engineer's.**
- GitHub user search with location filters: `location:India`, `location:Mumbai`,
  `location:Bengaluru` + `comfyui` / `lipsync` / `talking-head` / `video-generation`

### 11.6 The qualification bar - apply to every candidate

1. One artifact **>=60s**, one named **recurring character**, **synced dialogue**, **>=10 shots**
2. A **second** artifact showing the same character or the same process *(one good film can be luck; two proves process)*
3. A written or video **post-mortem** naming specific tools, failures and fixes
4. Evidence of **hands-on operation** - their own claim, credits, or published assets

**Two of four = interview. Three = strong. All four = rare; move fast.**

**Scoring rule: a post that documents a *failure* and its fix outranks a polished showcase every
time.** Wasserman's deliberate "rejected pot" negative control is the template.

The full stack — prompt craft + character-sheet discipline + a consistency method + voice cloning
